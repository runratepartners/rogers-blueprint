What we had wrong

The audit was built from the outside. Sunday fixed it.

Before the call we only had what Google, Instagram and the public records show. Four things were wrong or missing, and each one changes the plan. We leave them on the page so you can see we were listening.

Reframe 01

The financing is yours, and it is better than the one next door.

Roger's Acceptance, the finance company behind the store for 39 years, was the family's asset and the thing to build around.
Roger's Acceptance belongs to Roger, who still owns it and the building. Petra worked there 33 years. The financing Rogers Furniture customers actually get is Lizzy's own company: about 100 accounts on the books, 25 or so active, 95% of applicants approved, 20% interest. "Their rate went up to 50%. You get a $1,000 loan and you pay like $1,999."

The audit's idea of adding a second outside lender is dead. You already have the best financing on the street and nobody can find it. The plan is to put Lizzy's approval in front of every customer online, in Spanish, before they walk in. The money from financing stays in the family instead of going next door.

Reframe 02

Custom sofas make the same margin as everything else. The win is volume, not price.

Custom work carries a fatter margin, so the plan was to charge against the custom specialists at $2,700 and up.
"If something costs 200, I sell it for 400. Same for custom. 50%." Builds take 7 days, two weeks at the most, three builders, "and they're all slow right now." Five custom sofas sold in the last three months. About three people a week ask for a specific size.

We stop trying to raise the custom price and start closing the people already asking. Three a week is roughly 12 a month asking, and five a quarter buying. Every one of those who walks out is a $2,000 ticket at your own margin, built by a shop that wants the work. That is the fastest money in the building, and it does not need a single new customer.

Reframe 03

Sebastian is the salesman in the reviews, and he already does the thing we were going to build.

Petra was the only person in the store and Sebastian worked construction.
Most of the five-star Google reviews name Sebastian. He already prints the customer's floor plan and places the sofa and table on it. "Customers just love him." He also drives the truck with a cousin, and said out loud: "Execution is definitely one thing I lack in. Good ideas will only get you so far."

We do not build a floor-plan tool from scratch. We take what Sebastian does by hand in the store and put it on the phone and the website, so the woman waiting on her apartment keys and the one who has to convince her husband get the picture by text, with a price and a monthly payment, in 15 minutes. Sebastian runs it. We run the follow-up he said he does not.

Reframe 04

You already have the list. It is on paper.

A store with no website and no email list has to buy every customer from scratch.
"We have 100, 150 good customers that we know of right now that have paid very, very well," plus "six years, if not ten years of paperwork of old customers." Some already come back. Some found you on Instagram. Some came because of the five stars on Google.

The cheapest sales in the next 60 days are not strangers. They are the people in those boxes, who already trust you, getting one text in Spanish before Thanksgiving. We type the list in. That job is ours.

What is actually wrong

Five things, in order.

The first one is the one you raised. The last one can wait, and we say so.

Critical · costing money today

Your customers stopped coming out, and there is nowhere else for them to find you.

Eighty percent of your customers speak Spanish, and right now many of them are staying home. A family in Baldwin Park that needs a sofa this month searches "muebles La Puente" on a phone, sees three stores with websites and prices, and never sees Rogers, because there is no website, the Google listing has no link, and the Instagram has no prices. They buy from whoever answered. This is the whole gap, and it is why September was $14,000 instead of $25,000.

Critical · costing money today

Three people a week ask for a custom size. Most of them leave with nothing, and you never hear from them again.

"I'm getting my apartment this week, I just have to get the keys and get measurements." "I love this one but I need to convince my husband." Both walk out, and there is no phone number saved, no text the next day, no picture of the sofa in their room. Five sold in three months against roughly 36 asking. Nobody on Hacienda advertises custom. "No one's used it as a niche." That is yours to take, and the people are already in your store.

High · costing money, less urgent

Ninety-five percent get approved at 20%, and no customer knows that until they are standing at the counter.

Roger's Acceptance next door charges 50%. Lizzy approves almost everyone with three options, half down, layaway, or low or no down. A customer afraid to come in cannot learn any of that from outside. The monthly payment is the number people buy on, "they don't care about this number, they care about this number," and it appears nowhere online.

High · costing money, less urgent

100 to 150 customers who paid well, and ten years of records, have never been asked to come back.

They are in paper files. Nobody has texted them a Christmas offer, a custom sofa deadline, or a thank you. A list like that is the one asset the big stores spend millions to build. Yours is in a filing cabinet.

Moderate · this can wait

Downsizing, the rent and free delivery.

Ten thousand square feet for $5,000 a month is a good deal, and you are using almost all of it. The competition's free delivery hurts, but Sebastian and a cousin on the truck is cheaper than any company right now. Do not touch the space or the truck until we have November and December numbers on January 15. If sales come back, the space is an asset for staging and storage. If they do not, it is a conversation with Roger, not a reason to move today.

Sales, custom orders, financing applications, WhatsApp leads, walk-ins captured, Google reviews, and which day and hour the customers actually come. You said Friday to Sunday, maybe Wednesday evening. In four weeks you will know, and the slow hours become postcard hours at the new apartments.
